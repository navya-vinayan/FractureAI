
X-RAY INFORMATION

An X-ray is an imaging test that uses a small amount of radiation to create pictures of bones and other structures inside the body.

HOW X-RAYS HELP IDENTIFY FRACTURES

- X-rays can show breaks or cracks in bones.
- They can help identify bone alignment.
- They can help doctors assess the location of a suspected fracture.
- Different views may be needed to assess an injury.

COMMON X-RAY VIEWS

1. AP view: Anteroposterior view, where the X-ray beam passes from front to back.
2. Lateral view: A side view of the body part.
3. Oblique view: An angled view that may show structures not clearly visible in other views.

LIMITATIONS OF X-RAYS

- Some small fractures may not be visible initially.
- Overlapping bones can make interpretation difficult.
- Image quality and positioning can affect visibility.
- Additional imaging may be needed when clinically indicated.

IMPORTANT

An AI fracture prediction system is only a screening aid. It cannot confirm or rule out a fracture. A qualified radiologist or doctor must interpret the X-ray and make the diagnosis.
